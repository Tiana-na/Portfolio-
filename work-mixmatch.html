CASE STUDY

A UX case study on turning wardrobe overwhelm into everyday, confident outfits.

01The Problem

A full closet.
Nothing to wear.

Many women stand in front of a full closet and still feel they have nothing to wear, not because they lack clothes, but because it's hard to see all the possibilities they already have.

50%

of what's already in her closet goes unworn.

02The Insight

More options.
Harder decisions.

The more options you have, the harder it becomes to decide. This is a well-known phenomenon in UX and the psychology of decision-making: choice overload creates difficulty, even when there's abundance.

Choice overload creates difficulty, even when there's abundance.

FEW CHOICESDECISION FATIGUE

05Brand Concept: Logo

Wardrobe architecture, not clothing.

Instead of representing clothing itself, it represents the invisible structure behind every organized wardrobe. Just as a wardrobe is carefully planned into functional compartments, Mix & Match helps users organize their clothing into meaningful combinations.

Shelves. Sections. Balance. Modularity.

07What Colors Signal

A palette that's calm and current everywhere.

The palette needed to feel like a contemporary, light digital product, never heavy, while still giving the user a sense of confidence and control.

Calm, by Design

Sky Blue dominates almost every page. Light blue reads as trust and mental clarity, the opposite of closet-induced anxiety. It quietly tells the user: this is handled, you can relax.

One Warm Voice

Rose is the only warm color in the core palette (blue, navy, emerald), so it stands out wherever it's used. It carries the human, expressive side that blue alone can't say.

A Quiet Bonus

Emerald reinforces the promise of rediscovering value in what you already own: a hint of renewal and sustainability, without turning the brand into an eco-lecture.

Grounding Weight

Navy anchors every screen — headlines, primary text, and structure all live here so the lighter accents can do their job.
