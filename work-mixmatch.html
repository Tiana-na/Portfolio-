A Full Closet. Nothing to Wear.

Case study

Brand identity, built from scratch, for an app that turns wardrobe overwhelm into everyday, confident outfits.

Role
Brand designer. Created the brand from scratch and built the full brand book.
Project
Academic project, revisited and refined after graduation.
Tools
Figma, InDesign, Gemini, Claude
01The Problem

A full closet.
Nothing to wear.

Many women stand in front of a full closet and still feel they have nothing to wear, not because they lack clothes, but because it's hard to see all the possibilities they already have.

1 in 4

of the clothes in the average wardrobe go unworn.

Source: WRAP (2022), rounded from 26%

02The Insight

More options.
Harder decisions.

The more options you have, the harder it becomes to decide. This is a well-known phenomenon in UX and the psychology of decision-making: choice overload creates difficulty, even when there’s abundance.

Choice overload creates difficulty, even when there’s abundance.

Sources: Iyengar & Lepper (2000) · Danziger et al. (2011)

03The Cycle

New clothes.
Same disconnect.

Social pressure keeps the pace of buying constant. Repeating an outfit rarely feels acceptable, so new pieces get bought without a clear view of what's already there, and the same gap repeats.

Source: Harris, Roby & Dibb (2016)

Mix & Match isn't about buying less. It's about buying smarter: making sure every piece, new or old, actually works with what you already own.

04Brand Promise

Helping you rediscover the value of the clothes you already own.

05Vision

A world where every wardrobe reaches its full potential.

Rediscover.
Simplify.
Wear more.
06Brand Concept: Logo

Wardrobe architecture, not clothing.

Instead of representing clothing itself, it represents the invisible structure behind every organized wardrobe. Just as a wardrobe is carefully planned into functional compartments, Mix & Match helps users organize their clothing into meaningful combinations.

Shelves. Sections. Balance. Modularity.

08What Colors Signal

A palette that's calm and current everywhere.

The palette needed to feel like a contemporary, light digital product, never heavy, while still giving the user a sense of confidence and control.

Calm, by Design

Sky Blue dominates almost every page. Light blue reads as trust and mental clarity, the opposite of closet-induced anxiety. It quietly tells the user: this is handled, you can relax.

One Warm Voice

Rose is the only warm color in the palette, so it always stands out. It carries the human, expressive side that blue alone can't say.

A Quiet Bonus

Emerald reinforces the promise of rediscovering value in what you already own: a hint of renewal and sustainability, without turning the brand into an eco-lecture.

Grounding Weight

Navy, used mostly for text, keeps the pastel palette from feeling childish. It's the anchor of seriousness behind all the softness.

Not a Gender Code

Bright blue and bright pink together risk reading as boys' blue, girls' pink. Instead of swapping the hue, we muted it: a dusty, terracotta-leaning rose pairs with blue as a considered, adult palette.

The result: a palette that's calm and current everywhere, human in exactly one place, and never coded by gender.
