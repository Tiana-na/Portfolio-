01 · Context

My Role

In my role at a B2B startup, I worked across both Creative and Customer Success. On the CS side, that meant day-to-day work with the platform on behalf of subscribers: helping with the interface, running usage walkthroughs, onboarding new subscribers, and more. The technical build itself was handled by the development team. I analyzed customer calls, support tickets, and live onboarding sessions to surface friction points among small and medium business owners. Alongside this, I also created visual content for businesses across different industries, from beauty and self-care to doctors, coaches and therapists, lawyers, and more, content built for digital platforms, including posts, landing pages, and brand websites. This case study focuses on the pain points and solutions that stood out most in subscribers' everyday use of the system.

02 · The Problem

Customers Were Bypassing the System

Didn't understand the status of their content request to the creative team
02
Had no way to submit a new content request through the interface
03
Couldn't answer in depth during the real-time discovery call
04
Didn't know how to communicate with the team through the in-app chat

Instead of using the platform, they bypassed it, creating unnecessary load on the team and hurting the user experience.

Thought Process

In a fast-paced startup environment, I chose a lightweight, real-data-driven approach over a formal user journey mapping process or polished wireframes. I identified recurring patterns directly from customer calls and inquiries, and drafted a solution direction in writing that was reviewed with the development team before moving forward.

The guiding logic stayed consistent across every solution. In most cases, customers weren't bypassing the system because they preferred other tools. They did it because the system didn't give them a visible or accessible enough answer. So every solution was first tested against this question: does it keep the customer inside the interface, instead of pushing them out of it.

Decisions also kept evolving based on real user response. The content-request button, for example, was evaluated with subscribers after launch, and since the feedback showed the process was too long, it was later streamlined into a simpler, shorter flow.

Each solution was handled independently, based on the situation at the time, rather than planned upfront as one unified initiative, but all of them stemmed from the same working hypothesis.

03 · Solution 1 / 4
✓ Implemented

Streamlining Content Request Tracking

The customer didn't reach out because they didn't understand. They reached out because they couldn't see.

Customers had no visibility into what was happening with the request they'd submitted to the creative team, and this lack of transparency created anxiety and repeat inquiries to the CS team. A clear status view was added directly in the interface.

Before
After

Repeat inquiries about request status dropped by roughly 20% after implementation.

03 · Solution 2 / 4
✓ Implemented

Improving How Content Requests Are Submitted

When the path to an action is too long, the user gives up.

The problem: There was no way to submit a new request through the interface, only an edit request for existing content selected from the gallery. Subscribers who wanted something entirely new reached out over WhatsApp, which created load on the CS team, added an unnecessary step before the request reached the creative team, and at one point accounted for roughly 40% of inbound inquiries.

The solution: A prominent primary button was added to the dashboard for submitting a content request digitally and independently. Every subscriber could now complete the action easily, which made them more self-sufficient and confident in the interface and reduced the load on the team. In addition, a quick option was added to submit an edit request directly from the content itself, instead of through the unintuitive "Help" button, which also cut down on the related inquiries that used to go through WhatsApp.

Next up: Following feedback from subscribers that the process is too long and cumbersome, it will be streamlined into a simpler, shorter flow.

03 · Solution 3 / 4
✓ Implemented

Streamlining the Client Discovery Process

Questions about brand identity require thought, not a quick response.

The initial discovery process included complex questions about brand voice and differentiation, and required a 15–30 minute call that depended on the client's availability and openness, which often meant we got shallow answers. This hurt the accuracy of the content produced, created repeat conversations with subscribers, and drained the workflow while also causing frustration. After user research with subscribers, I converted the process into a digital form the client fills out after joining, with all the information saved to their profile.

Before
After

The move to a form created a natural filter: most clients completed the process independently, freeing up the team’s time to focus on those who genuinely needed a deeper discovery call.

03 · Solution 4 / 4
Proposed · Awaiting prioritization

Improving Chat Discoverability in the Interface

Customers used what felt familiar and safe. They couldn't find the interface's chat.

The in-app chat existed, but no one saw it, since it was tucked away in a hidden menu, so customers reached out over WhatsApp because that's what they knew. To get customers operating within the interface, I proposed moving chat access to a prominent, familiar location. The choice of a floating position came from the fact that it's a familiar user behavior from other interfaces. There was no need to teach users a new pattern, just to lean on an intuitive process they already knew, used, and trusted.

04 · Outcome

The Common Thread Across All Solutions

Some solutions were implemented in full. Others remain prioritized on the product roadmap. The single redesigned home screen simultaneously solved 3 of the 4 identified problems (request status, creating a new request, and reaching the team), demonstrating that one focused solution can replace several separate fixes.

Fewer repeat inquiries

Reduced load on the CS team by surfacing information directly in the interface

More accurate discovery

A digital form produced deeper answers and more genuine marketing content

Communication through the right channels

Reduced off-platform communication, redirected back to the tools built for it

Every solution was grounded in real data gathered from the field. Direct access to the customer is the right starting point for any improvement to the user experience.
